---
title: Case scenario on COPD with cor pulmonale
---

A 55 year old male, smoker, presented with intermittent respiratory distress & cough for 3 years and bipedal swelling for 3 days. Examination revealed raised JVP, bilateral extensive rhonchi on auscultation of both lung field, hepatomegaly.

Based on the clinical features you've outlined, the most likely diagnosis is **Chronic Bronchitis with Cor Pulmonale**.

Here is a concise breakdown of why these findings lead to that conclusion:

### Diagnostic Justification

The patient is a middle-aged smoker presenting with chronic respiratory symptoms and acute signs of right-sided heart failure.

1.  **Chronic Obstructive Airway Disease:** The history of intermittent cough and respiratory distress (dyspnea) for three years in a 55-year-old smoker strongly suggests Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD), which includes chronic bronchitis and emphysema.
2.  **Chronic Bronchitis Features:** The physical finding of **bilateral extensive rhonchi on both lung fields** is characteristic of chronic bronchitis, where plenty of rhonchi are present throughout inspiration and expiration.
3.  **Cor Pulmonale:** Cor pulmonale is defined as right ventricular hypertrophy with or without failure, secondary to diseases of the lung parenchyma or pulmonary vessels. The development of **bipedal swelling** (dependent edema), **raised JVP**, and **hepatomegaly** indicates right ventricular failure (right-sided heart failure). This heart failure is secondary to the underlying lung disease (chronic bronchitis).

This entire presentation aligns with the classic phenotype of the **"Blue Bloater"** (seen in chronic bronchitis), characterized by the patient being cyanosed (blue) and edematous (bloater), with cough and sputum being the main features, and pulmonary hypertension/cor pulmonale developing early on.

### Vital Points to Remember

*   **Rhonchi vs. Crackles:** The prominence of rhonchi distinguishes chronic bronchitis (airway obstruction due to secretions and spasm) from conditions like left ventricular failure (where bilateral basal crepitations/crackles are common and rhonchi are minimal).
*   **Cor Pulmonale Definition:** Always remember that the failure is *secondary* to the lung disease, which is the key distinction of cor pulmonale.
*   **Hepatomegaly in Failure:** Hepatomegaly is a sign of systemic venous congestion and right heart failure. The pain felt in the right upper abdomen, if present, can be due to liver pain caused by congestion.
*   **Age and Smoking:** Both age (>40 years) and smoking history are major risk factors for chronic obstructive pulmonary disease.

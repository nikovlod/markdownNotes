---
title: Different types of fragrances
---

To understand the world of fragrances, it is helpful to look at them through four distinct lenses: **Concentration** (how strong they are), **Olfactory Families** (what they smell like), **The Fragrance Pyramid** (how they evolve over time), and **Market Types** (who makes them). 

Here is an in-depth explanation of the different types of fragrances.

---

### 1. Types by Concentration (The Strength)
A fragrance is essentially a mixture of raw aromatic oils (natural and synthetic) diluted in a solvent, typically alcohol and water. The ratio of perfume oil to alcohol determines the fragrance's longevity, projection (how far it radiates), and sillage (the scent trail it leaves behind).

*   **Parfum (Extrait de Parfum)**
    *   **Concentration:** 20% to 30% (sometimes up to 40%).
    *   **Longevity:** 8 to 12+ hours.
    *   **Characteristics:** This is the most concentrated and expensive form of perfumery. Because it has less alcohol, it sits closer to the skin, meaning it has lower projection but incredibly high longevity. It is generally safer for sensitive skin.
*   **Eau de Parfum (EDP)**
    *   **Concentration:** 15% to 20%.
    *   **Longevity:** 6 to 8 hours.
    *   **Characteristics:** This is the most popular concentration in modern perfumery. It offers an excellent balance between longevity, projection, and price. EDPs typically highlight the heart and base notes of a fragrance.
*   **Eau de Toilette (EDT)**
    *   **Concentration:** 5% to 15%.
    *   **Longevity:** 4 to 6 hours.
    *   **Characteristics:** EDT is lighter and often focuses more on the brighter, volatile top notes. It is incredibly popular for daytime wear, office environments, and warmer weather because it projects well initially but doesn't become cloying or overwhelming.
*   **Eau de Cologne (EDC)**
    *   **Concentration:** 2% to 4%.
    *   **Longevity:** 2 to 3 hours.
    *   **Characteristics:** Often just called "Cologne," this concentration is highly diluted with alcohol. Traditional EDCs are predominantly citrus-based and are designed to be applied liberally for a quick, refreshing burst of scent, rather than all-day wear. 
*   **Eau Fraîche / Body Mists**
    *   **Concentration:** 1% to 3%.
    *   **Longevity:** 1 to 2 hours.
    *   **Characteristics:** Similar concentration to EDC, but Eau Fraîche is diluted primarily with water rather than alcohol. These are used as light, hydrating refreshers.

---

### 2. Types by Olfactory Family (The Scent Profile)
In 1992, fragrance expert Michael Edwards developed the "Fragrance Wheel" to categorize scents. Most fragrances fall into one of four primary families, though modern perfumery often blends them.

*   **Floral**
    *   The most popular and broadest family. It can range from a *Soliflore* (the scent of a single flower, like a pure rose) to a *Bouquet* (a complex blend of jasmine, lily, tuberose, and peony). 
    *   *Sub-categories:* Soft Floral (powdery, aldehydes), Floral Oriental (sweet, subtly spicy).
*   **Amber / Oriental**
    *   Known for being warm, rich, sensual, and exotic. These fragrances rely heavily on ingredients like vanilla, myrrh, frankincense, amber, cinnamon, and cardamom. They are typically worn in the evening or during winter.
    *   *Sub-categories:* Soft Oriental (incense, warm flowers), Woody Oriental (earthy, spicy).
*   **Woody**
    *   Earthy, dry, and warm. These are predominantly built on notes of sandalwood, cedarwood, vetiver, and patchouli. They are highly common in traditional masculine perfumery but are increasingly popular in unisex and feminine scents.
    *   *Sub-categories:* Mossy Woods (earthy, sweet), Dry Woods (smoky, leathery).
*   **Fresh**
    *   Uplifting, zesty, and cooling. This family is perfect for spring and summer.
    *   *Sub-categories:* **Citrus** (lemon, bergamot, mandarin), **Aquatic/Water** (marine notes, sea salt, rain—often created using a synthetic molecule called Calone), and **Green** (crushed leaves, fresh-cut grass, galbanum).

**Specialty Sub-Families:**
*   **Gourmand:** Scents that smell "edible." They feature notes like chocolate, caramel, coffee, cotton candy, and almond. 
*   **Fougère (Fern):** A classic masculine category. It is a strict structural accord featuring a bright citrus top (usually bergamot), a floral/herbaceous heart (lavender), and a mossy, woody base (oakmoss and coumarin). It smells like a classic, high-end barbershop.
*   **Chypre (Cyprus):** A complex, sophisticated category built on a contrast between fresh top notes (bergamot) and a deep, earthy base (oakmoss, patchouli, and labdanum).

### 3. Types by the Fragrance Pyramid (How Scent Evolves)
Fragrances are not static; they are formulated to evaporate in three stages over time. Understanding this prevents you from buying a fragrance based solely on the first sniff.

*   **Top Notes (Head Notes):** 
    *   **Duration:** First 15 to 30 minutes.
    *   **Function:** The introduction. These are made of small, highly volatile molecules that evaporate quickly. 
    *   **Common Notes:** Citruses (lemon, bergamot), light fruits (berries, grapefruit), and fresh herbs (mint, basil).
*   **Heart Notes (Middle Notes):**
    *   **Duration:** 30 minutes to 3 hours.
    *   **Function:** The core personality of the fragrance. Once the top notes fade, the heart emerges to act as a bridge, masking the initial harshness of the base notes.
    *   **Common Notes:** Florals (rose, jasmine, lavender), spices (cinnamon, nutmeg), and heavy greens.
*   **Base Notes:**
    *   **Duration:** 4 to 12+ hours.
    *   **Function:** The foundation and memory of the perfume. These are large, heavy molecules that evaporate very slowly. They act as "fixatives," helping to hold onto the lighter top and heart notes.
    *   **Common Notes:** Woods (cedar, sandalwood), musk, vanilla, amber, and leather.

---

### 4. Types by Market Category (The Creators)
Finally, fragrances are categorized by the business models of the houses that create them, which often dictates their style, price, and exclusivity.

*   **Designer Fragrances:**
    *   Created by mainstream fashion brands (e.g., Chanel, Dior, Tom Ford, Versace, Yves Saint Laurent).
    *   **Characteristics:** These are designed to be "mass-appealing"—created to be loved by the widest variety of people possible. They are widely accessible, well-marketed, and tend to follow (or set) current mainstream trends.
*   **Niche Fragrances:**
    *   Created by houses dedicated solely to making perfume (e.g., Creed, Amouage, Le Labo, Parfums de Marly, Maison Francis Kurkdjian).
    *   **Characteristics:** More expensive and exclusive. Niche perfumers often take greater artistic risks, creating challenging, unique scent profiles that might not appeal to everyone. They often boast higher-quality, rare, or strictly natural raw materials.
*   **Indie Fragrances:**
    *   Created by independent, often self-taught artisan perfumers who own, formulate, and bottle everything themselves (e.g., Zoologist, Imaginary Authors, Kerosene).
    *   **Characteristics:** Highly experimental, avant-garde, and strictly small-batch. Indie perfumes are where you will find the most unconventional scents (e.g., perfumes designed to smell like a bat cave, a thunderstorm, or a specific memory). 

By understanding these four categories, you can easily decode fragrance terminology—for instance, knowing that a *"Niche Woody EDP"* will likely be a long-lasting, high-quality, earthy scent with a unique twist that isn't found at the local department store.
